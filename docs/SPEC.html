Position & principles.

§ 00

Most academic lab sites are a list of papers with a photo on top. This one argues a position and then shows its work. The position is the lab's thesis: every gain in AI for code is paid for somewhere — in compute, in energy, in the ability to see why the model did what it did — and the AURA Lab measures that price before claiming the gain.

Five rules the whole site obeys

  1. Every figure names its source. No number appears without a caption saying where it came from and when it was collected. Figures the lab cannot source are not drawn.
  2. The record is machine-maintained. Publications, counts and artifact links are produced by the sync pipeline (§ 10), never typed by hand. Hand-typed numbers rot.
  3. Shape over score. The site shows trends and composition — output per year, venue mix, uptake direction — and does not headline a citation index (§ 08).
  4. Artifacts are first class. A replication package is displayed with the same weight as the paper it belongs to. Reproducibility is presented as a baseline, not a feature.
  5. People are named, not tiled. Every person on the site links to a profile with their own papers and threads. Nobody is a decorative headshot.

04.2 — Research index & thread pages

The index repeats the homepage's single axis at full width, then gives each thread a half-page block: letter, title, the short line, its tag row, its paper count, and its two most recent papers. A thread page is: hero (letter at 180 px, title, short, tags), then a two-column body — the essay from the Markdown file on the left, an aside on the right carrying the people on this thread and an “In numbers” list (papers / reviewed / preprints) — then every paper in the thread with the featured keys first, then prev/next thread links that wrap around.

Thread membership is assigned automatically by the classifier in scripts/pubs/areas.py from the keywords array in each thread's frontmatter, matched against title, abstract and keywords. The page states this in one line so nobody mistakes it for a curated claim. 39 of 52 entries currently classify; the rest are IEEE Computer columns and are correctly left unassigned.

04.3 — Publications /publications/ & paper pages

The homepage section and the index share one design, and it is only the papers. The separate “research output” section is gone, and so are the three figures that stood in for it — volume, uptake and venue mix. They were an abstraction of the list sitting on top of the list. The venue is now carried where it belongs, on each paper. Order on the page is: header, filter bar, papers.

THE PAPER CARD

Each paper is led by the first page of its PDF, rendered at build time and normalised to a 17:22 frame, top-aligned so the title block and author list are what you see. Then five things in a fixed order: the venue of acceptance as a chip in that venue's hue with the year (ICPC 2026, TOSEM 2026, arXiv 2026) and the kind beside it; the title; the authors with lab members in bold; and the link row — PDF · DOI · ARXIV · BIBTEX · ARTIFACT, always in that order so the eye can find one without reading. The preview is a <button>, not a link: activating it opens the reader rather than navigating away. A paper with no open full text gets a typographic tile instead of a preview, so the grid stays even.

ALIGNMENT IS STRUCTURAL, NOT COSMETIC

Titles are different lengths, so left to themselves every card's author line and link row sit at a different height and the band reads as noise. The card is therefore five rows of a CSS subgrid — preview, venue, title, authors, links — sharing the grid's row tracks with every other card in its band. Every venue chip in a row sits on one baseline, every title block starts on one baseline, every link row lands on one baseline, and nothing is truncated to achieve it. There is a flex fallback under @supports not (grid-template-rows: subgrid).

PAGINATION — THE PAGE IS WHAT THE SCREEN HOLDS

A wall of paper first pages is impressive once and tiring thereafter, so the grid never runs past what the viewport can show. Page size is not a fixed number: width picks the columns, height picks the rows, both declared in CSS, and the script multiplies them. It reads the resolved grid-template-columns for the column count and a --rows custom property for the row count, so every breakpoint decision stays in the stylesheet.

Two rows is the norm. A tall, narrow window — a portrait monitor, a half-screen browser — gets a third row and a much taller page image, because the vertical space is there and a 2:1 masthead sliver would waste it. A wide desktop keeps two rows and simply shows more columns.

THE WHOLE PAGE, NEVER A CROP

The preview is the entire first page — title block, authors, abstract, the start of the introduction, the arXiv stamp down the spine. Not a masthead band, not a top slice. A reader recognises a paper by the shape of its first page, and a cropped strip throws that away. aspect-ratio: 17/22 with object-fit: contain, so the page is never clipped at any column width.

That is a tall card, so the row count absorbs the cost instead of the image: width picks the columns, height picks how many rows of full pages the screen can actually hold, both declared in CSS and read by the pager as columns × --rows. A laptop gets one row of four; a 5K gets two rows of seven; a portrait monitor two rows of three. Nobody gets a cropped page.

If the section needs to be shorter, take a row, take a column, trim the metadata, hide the lead — the page image is the one thing that stays whole.

THE READER

Activating a preview opens the full PDF in place, so a visitor reads the paper without being handed off to Scholar or a publisher. It is a role="dialog" aria-modal="true" overlay: ink backdrop at 0.86 with a 4 px blur, a panel at most 1180 px wide and 94 vh tall, and an <iframe> loading <pdf>#view=FitH. The header carries venue · year · kind, the title, and three controls — arXiv ↗, PDF ↗, close.

  • Escape closes it, so does clicking the backdrop, so does the close button.
  • Focus moves into the dialog on open, Tab is trapped inside it, and focus returns to the originating preview on close.
  • body.locked stops the page behind from scrolling; the iframe is destroyed on close so nothing keeps streaming.
  • If the frame has not loaded in 8 s the panel replaces the spinner with a plain “open in a new tab” link — no permanent spinner, ever.
WHY ONLY 24 OF 52Only papers with an open full text can be embedded. arXiv sends no X-Frame-Options and no frame-ancestors, so its PDFs frame cleanly; ACM DL and IEEE Xplore are paywalled and frame-blocked, and always will be. The durable fix is green open access: post the author's accepted manuscript — which ACM and IEEE author agreements both permit — to /papers/<key>.pdf on this site. Then every paper is same-origin, always framable, always fast, and the lab is not depending on a third party to stay readable. That is a per-paper upload, not an engineering problem.

THE INDEX PAGE

/publications/ is the same grid without the homepage's five-card ceiling, grouped by year, with three filter axes — bucket, year and research area — as client-side chip toggles operating on data-* attributes, so the page works with JavaScript off and filters with it on. aura.bib downloads the whole record.

A single paper page carries: venue badge and date, title, full author line (lab members linked to their profiles), abstract, a link row (PDF · DOI · arXiv · Code · Data · Slides · Video, each rendered only if present), a copyable BibTeX block, the replication package if there is one, the research areas it belongs to, and its per-paper citation count with the source named — which is where citation numbers are allowed to live (§ 08).

Space, rules and motion

  • Shell — max-width 1440px, --pad 44 px → 20 px at ≤640.
  • Section rhythm — 66 px top margin, 1.5 px --rule-3 top rule, 18 px to the first content row. Rules are structural: hairline --rule separates rows, 1 px --rule-2 bounds cards, 1.5 px solid ink opens a section. Nothing has a border-radius above 2 px, and there are no shadows except the mega panel's.
  • Breakpoints — 1180 (nav collapses, grids halve), 760 (bar simplifies, CTA moves to drawer), 700 (record rows go block), 640 (single column, pad drops), 440 (portrait band → 2-up).
  • Motion — the sequence above, plus the 3 s status-dot pulse and the 0.18 s burger morph. Nothing is parked invisible waiting on an observer; the resting state is the finished state.

Photography.

§ 07

The lab's real photographs are candid and environmental — people outdoors, in doorways, holding cats. That is an asset, not a problem: it reads as a real group rather than a stock faculty page. It just has to be handled consistently.

  1. Face-detect and crop. Nothing else. Each portrait is cropped to a 26:34 frame with the face at ~28.5 % of the frame height, sitting 42 % from the top. Detection is YuNet (opencv_zoo), so the crop is deterministic and re-running gives the same frame. Then a resize to 560 × 730 and that is the whole pipeline.
  2. No processing of any kind. No exposure normalisation, no background separation, no vignette, no sharpening, no saturation change. The photographs go out as they were taken.
  3. Serve at 2×. 560 × 730 source displayed at 260 × 340, through Astro's image pipeline as AVIF with a WebP fallback. Never inline base64 in production — that is a mockup-only device.

What replaces the citation index

Six things, and together they are a stronger argument than one number: the venue mix (where the work lands), the output growth (whether the lab is accelerating), the artifact count (whether the work can be checked), the software (whether anyone uses what it builds), the service record (whether the field asks it to judge), and the awards. Four Distinguished Paper–class recognitions in two years say more than a citation total for a lab founded in 2024, because a young lab's citation total is structurally low and says nothing about quality.

Author identity

The pipeline finds a member's papers through the ids block in their Markdown frontmatter — DBLP pid, Google Scholar id, one or more Semantic Scholar ids, OpenAlex id, ORCID, an arxiv: true flag, and free-text aliases for name spellings. A member with no ids can still be matched inside a paper's author list, but their solo papers are invisible to the pipeline. That is the single most common cause of a missing paper.

OPEN — SIX MEMBERS HAVE NO DBLP IDThe sync report names them and gives the exact pid to paste: Saima Afrin 367/9210, Aya Garryyeva 399/3436, Joseph Call 399/7150, Afia Farjana 341/6665, Md Zahidul Haque Alvi 362/1508, Zaiyu Cheng 386/5272. Six one-line edits, and their independent work starts appearing.

Merge rules

  • Records cluster by DOI, then arXiv id, then normalised title, using union-find.
  • Within a cluster, each field is taken from the highest-priority source that has it (PRIORITY in merge.py); type is resolved separately by TYPE_PRIORITY, so a paper indexed as a preprint on arXiv and as a journal article on Crossref resolves to journal.
  • A previously-stored record is dropped when any live source has the paper, so corrections upstream propagate instead of persisting.
  • volume is nulled for preprints — CoRR's abs/2507.09665 is not a volume number, and it leaked into reviewed records before this rule existed.
  • overrides.yml wins over everything, and every override should carry a comment saying why.

The publications pipeline

Six sources, queried in this order, each with its own failure mode already handled: DBLP (pid XML, with mirror fallback and a search-API path for when every record endpoint 503s), Crossref, Semantic Scholar (author and batch endpoints have different allowed field sets), OpenAlex, arXiv Atom (author queries need the full given name — au:Mastropaolo_Antonio, not au:Mastropaolo_A), and the local BibTeX file. Google Scholar via scholarly is opt-in behind --scholar and is not run in CI, because it is rate-limited and unreliable — it is a manual top-up, not a dependency.

npm run sync:pubs -- --sources dblp,crossref,s2,openalex,arxiv,local
                    --scholar      opt-in Google Scholar pass
                    --offline      rebuild from cache only
                    --max-age 7    cache TTL in days
                    --dry-run      report, write nothing
                    --no-news      skip news drafting
                    --discover     suggest author IDs for members who lack them

The module is scripts/pubs/web.py, not http.py — the latter shadows the standard library's http package and breaks requests. Do not rename it back.

News drafting

A new peer-reviewed paper produces a Markdown file in src/content/news/ with draft: true, the paper key, the member slugs, and a one-line headline. It is invisible until a human sets draft: false, and it only posts to social if they also set social: true. Two switches, both defaulting to silence — the pipeline can be wrong, and being wrong quietly is much better than being wrong publicly.

Build-time generation

  • OG cards — one per page, rendered by satori and @resvg/resvg-js, carrying the page title, the lab mark and the section label. Nodes with empty children must pass undefined, not [], or satori throws on missing display.
  • Feeds — /rss.xml (news) and /publications.xml (the record).
  • Sitemap — @astrojs/sitemap, filtered to exclude /og/ and /drafts/.
  • Search index — a JSON blob of title, authors, venue, year and abstract, built alongside the record.

SEO & discovery.

§ 11

The goal Antonio actually asked for — augmenting the lab's presence in the research community — is mostly won here, not in the visual design. Three mechanisms:

1 · GOOGLE SCHOLAR INDEXING

Every paper page emits the citation_* meta tags Scholar's crawler requires: citation_title, one citation_author per author in order, citation_publication_date, citation_journal_title or citation_conference_title, citation_volume, citation_firstpage, citation_doi, citation_arxiv_id, and citation_pdf_url pointing at a directly-downloadable PDF. This is what makes the lab's own pages a Scholar source rather than a dead end, and it is the highest-leverage item on this page.

Accessibility & performance.

§ 12 · BUDGETS

ACCESSIBILITY — NON-NEGOTIABLE

  • Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 for body text, ≥ 3:1 for large text and UI borders, in both themes. The pairing to watch is --ink-5 on --surface — it is caption-only and must not carry meaning alone.
  • Every figure is an <svg role="img"> with an aria-label that states the actual numbers, so the data survives without sight. Fig. 3's label says explicitly that it shows direction only.
  • Colour never carries meaning alone: thread hues are always paired with the letter, venue tiers with the count, status with a word.
  • Visible focus ring — 2 px --live, 3 px offset — on every interactive element. The mega panels open on :focus-within, so they are reachable by keyboard.
  • Skip link to #main; one h1 per page; headings never skip a level.
  • prefers-reduced-motion disables both animations.
