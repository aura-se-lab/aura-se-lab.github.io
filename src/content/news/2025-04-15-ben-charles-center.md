Congratulations to [Ben Tremblay](/people/benjamin-tremblay/) on receiving William & Mary's **Charles Center Summer Research Grant**, which funds a summer of full-time undergraduate research.

Ben is an undergraduate in Computer Science specialising in AI/ML, working with Prof. Mastropaolo on large code models. His research sits where software engineering meets optimization: the models behind tools like GitHub Copilot are expensive to train and expensive to serve, and as they scale toward trillions of parameters the question of how to shrink them stops being an optimization detail and becomes a condition of using them at all.

The techniques he works with are the standard ones — parameter-efficient fine-tuning, quantization, pruning, adapter methods such as LoRA and QLoRA. The question he is asking about them is less standard, and it is the one the lab keeps returning to: **when an optimization method alters a model's underlying topology, what happens to code quality beyond correctness?** A compressed model may still pass its tests while producing code that is harder to maintain, less readable, or quietly less secure — and a benchmark that reports only functional correctness will show none of that.

The grant supports a summer of building the evaluation to answer it properly. Well deserved, Ben.
