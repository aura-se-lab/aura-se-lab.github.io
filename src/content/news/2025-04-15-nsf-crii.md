The National Science Foundation has funded our CRII proposal, *SHF: Advancing Sustainable Software Engineering Practices with Energy-Efficient Large Language Models for Code* ([award #2451058](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/showAward?AWD_ID=2451058)). Antonio Mastropaolo is sole PI; the award runs from **1 June 2025 to 31 May 2027**.

The CRII programme exists to support faculty early in their independent careers, and this one funds the line the lab has been building toward since it opened: reducing the compute, energy and carbon cost of training and deploying large language models for code, without giving up what makes them worth deploying.

Two levers carry the work. The first is **task-aware training-data optimization** — the observation that a great deal of what these models are trained on contributes nothing, and that identifying which parts matter is cheaper than processing all of it. The second is **model compression**, principally quantization, applied and evaluated specifically for software engineering rather than borrowed wholesale from general NLP.

What makes it a research problem rather than an engineering one is the measurement. Cheaper is easy to demonstrate and easy to overclaim; showing that a compressed, retrained model is still right — and still writes code someone can maintain — requires evaluation that most of the compression literature does not report. Building that evaluation is part of the award.
